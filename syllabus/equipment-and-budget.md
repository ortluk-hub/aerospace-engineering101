# Equipment and spending plan

## First session: no purchases
Use a ruler, notebook, calculator/phone, a cylindrical household object, and a scale if already available. An absent scale is a documented measurement limitation, not a reason to stop.

## Before the first powered flight
Compare the delivered total for:
- A reusable complete launch set with a simple single-stage rocket.
- Manufacturer-recommended certified motors, igniters, recovery protection, and batteries.
- Required adhesives or other assembly consumables.
- Travel and any site or club costs.

The initial planning target is approximately $50–70, not a verified shopping quote. No product or vendor has been selected. Check current prices and shipping before spending. Borrowing a club launch system may reduce upfront cost if practical.

Prefer a visible, recoverable baseline rocket and small manufacturer-approved motor over maximum advertised altitude. Match motor delay, loaded mass, launch guide, field, and wind to the actual vehicle.

## Later spending gates
Buy an altimeter only when optical/video uncertainty limits the experiment; confirm its fit, mass, range, and mounting first. Buy data logging components only after specifying the quantity, sampling rate, accuracy, and integration constraints. No 3D printer, telemetry, GPS, or premium kit is required initially.

Keep a ledger: date, item, purpose, delivered price, reusable/consumable, and flights supported. Record per-flight consumable cost separately.
