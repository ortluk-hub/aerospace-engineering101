# AE-101: Experimental Rocket and Spaceflight Engineering

## Scope and pace
A 15-module introductory undergraduate-style survey, self-paced. Allow roughly 4–6 hours per module: reading, independent calculations, a shared lab or simulation, and notebook review. Harder modules may take longer. This workload is not a claim of university credit equivalence. A full aerospace degree treats mechanics, fluids, thermodynamics, structures, controls, and orbital mechanics in separate courses.

Start with algebra, unit conversion, and right-triangle trigonometry. Introduce derivatives as rates of change, integrals as accumulated quantities, and numerical integration before relying on them. Optional extensions provide calculus derivations.

## Sequence

| Module | Subject | Evidence of learning |
|---|---|---|
| 01 | Measurement, uncertainty, Newtonian mechanics | Measurement report, force calculation, independent predictions |
| 02 | Vectors and equations of motion | Vertical-flight hand model; time-step convergence check |
| 03 | Motors and thrust curves | Integrate certified motor data; distinguish impulse from thrust |
| 04 | Stability and recovery | Loaded CG, estimated CP, static margin, recovery review |
| 05 | Aerodynamics | Drag and dynamic pressure calculations; sensitivity study |
| 06 | Flight Lab I | Approved baseline flight plan and documented observations |
| 07 | Experimental analysis | Uncertainty budget; compare measurements with predictions |
| 08 | Thermodynamics and propulsion | Energy, mass flow, nozzle concepts, specific impulse |
| 09 | Rocket equation and staging | Derivation with assumptions; staged mass-budget comparison |
| 10 | Atmosphere and high-speed flight | Density, speed of sound, Mach; limits of model similarity |
| 11 | Trajectory and flight dynamics | Gravity/drag losses, weathercocking, recovery drift |
| 12 | Flight Lab II | Change one approved variable; assess repeatability |
| 13 | Orbital mechanics | Circular orbit, energy, escape velocity, inclination |
| 14 | Mission design | Hohmann transfer and ideal delta-v budget |
| 15 | Capstone | Design review, simulation, construction, flight, defence |

Module 1 is complete. Modules 2–15 are planned, not yet authored.

## Working rhythm
Each person records an initial hypothesis independently. Work through theory and a hand calculation, then compare with simulation. Save predictions before any experiment. Collect observations without editing them to match expectations. Compare explanations afterward and record the next test.

Alternate calculation lead and measurement lead. At a launch, explicitly assign launch operator and observer; both may call a stop. Rotate between flights, never during a launch sequence.

## Review and assessment
Use a portfolio rather than competitive marks: independent problem sets, shared lab reports, simulation files, and a capstone review. Completion means both learners can explain assumptions and units, reproduce the calculation, describe uncertainty, and defend the conclusion. Incorrect predictions with honest analysis are valuable evidence.

Flight milestones require the flight rules and manufacturer requirements to be satisfied. Weather or site restrictions can postpone flight without postponing study. No powered flight in Module 1.

## Connections
For each module, connect one calculation to a real space programme. Compare engineering constraints and evidence rather than ranking programmes. Later topics include structural loads and controls as introductions; practical active guidance and high-power rocketry are outside this opening course.
