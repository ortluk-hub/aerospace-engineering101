# Learning objectives
By the end, each learner should be able to:
1. Convert engineering units, distinguish mass from weight, and report measurement uncertainty.
2. Draw force diagrams and build a simple time-dependent flight model with explicit assumptions.
3. Interpret a motor thrust curve and calculate total impulse and thrust-to-weight ratio.
4. Explain CG, CP, static margin, and the limits of static stability alone.
5. Estimate drag and dynamic pressure, and explain why simulation inputs matter.
6. Prepare a flight plan, measure what available instruments can actually resolve, and retain raw data.
7. Compare prediction with observation using uncertainty rather than treating every difference as a failure.
8. Explain specific impulse and derive the ideal rocket equation from momentum conservation.
9. Estimate staging benefits while accounting for structural mass.
10. Explain atmospheric effects, weathercocking, and recovery drift.
11. Calculate simple orbital velocities, energies, and transfer requirements.
12. Design and defend a modest model rocket with traceable requirements and evidence.

No single model-rocket flight demonstrates orbital mechanics, vacuum propulsion performance, or supersonic similarity. Those topics use calculations and simulations.
