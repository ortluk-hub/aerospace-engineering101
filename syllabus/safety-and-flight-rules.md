# Safety and flight rules

Read the complete [NAR Model Rocket Safety Code](https://www.nar.org/ModelRocketSafetyCode) and the vehicle, motor, and launcher instructions before each flight campaign. Source checked 2026-10-03. This course summary does not replace them.

## Hands-on scope
Use lightweight conventional model rockets, certified commercial motors used unchanged, electrical ignition with a safety interlock, and a suitable recovery system. Follow the manufacturer’s motor and delay recommendations.

## Flight gate
Before launch, both learners record agreement that:
- The landowner permits launching and recovery; applicable local rules, current fire restrictions, and airspace requirements have been checked.
- The field accommodates the motor and predicted recovery drift, with clear visibility and no aircraft conflict.
- Weather meets the safety code and any stricter manufacturer/site limit.
- Construction, motor retention, launch-guide fit, loaded stability, and recovery preparation have been checked.
- The launch operator, observer, countdown, spectator boundary, and misfire procedure are understood.

Do not fly toward people or targets, into clouds, near aircraft, or with hazardous payloads. Do not recover from power lines or other dangerous places.

## Opening A–C flights
NAR minimum field dimensions are 100 ft for A, 200 ft for B, and 400 ft for C. Spectator separation for D and smaller is at least 15 ft. These are minima, not a guarantee of containment; use a larger field when predictions or conditions require it. The code’s maximum wind is 20 mph; beginner flights should wait for substantially calmer conditions.

After a misfire, disable the electrical system and wait at least 60 seconds after the last attempt before approaching. Keep the launch guide near vertical, use a blast deflector, protect against rod-tip eye injury, and keep combustible vegetation away from the pad. Use flame-resistant recovery protection.

## Course practice
Either learner may stop a flight. Simulation is evidence for review, not flight authorization. Record unresolved safety questions as a no-go. Certified motors remain unmodified; no propellant manufacture or motor fabrication forms part of this course.
