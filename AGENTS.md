# Course authoring guidance
Preserve this as a low-cost father–son aerospace course for two adult learners, one aged 19 with substantial space-program and astronomy knowledge. Teach quantitatively without assuming calculus fluency. Explain notation in plain English.

Write complete lessons progressively; distinguish ready material from planned modules. Require independent predictions before solutions. Never invent measurements, purchased equipment, flights, permissions, sources, or completed assignments. Preserve notebooks and raw data; do not overwrite learner work or use destructive Git operations.

Every lesson needs objectives, prerequisites, theory, a worked example, independent problems, a practical activity, and a completion gate. Cite primary sources for technical claims. Explain assumptions, coordinate conventions, units, and uncertainty. Separate simulated, illustrative, and measured values.

Use certified commercial model rocket motors as manufactured, with manufacturer instructions and current NAR safety guidance. Check current site permission, fire restrictions, weather, and applicable airspace requirements before actual flights. No homemade motors or propellant work. Hardware spending follows demonstrated experimental need. Do not publish personal locations/contact details without explicit authorization.
