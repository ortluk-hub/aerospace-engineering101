# Aerospace Engineering 101
## A father–son experimental aerospace course

A self-paced, undergraduate-style introduction to rocket and spaceflight engineering for two adult learners. Our laboratory is an inexpensive reusable model rocket; our method is **predict → calculate → simulate → measure → explain → revise**.

This is an introductory survey with engineering labs, not an accredited course or a substitute for a full aerospace degree. Algebra and basic trigonometry are enough to start; calculus is introduced as needed. No equipment purchase is required for the first lesson.

### Start here
1. Read the [course outline](syllabus/course-outline.md) and [flight rules](syllabus/safety-and-flight-rules.md).
2. Complete [Module 1](modules/01-measurement-and-mechanics/README.md).
3. Work the [first problem set](problems/problem-sets/01-measurement-and-mechanics.md) independently before opening its solutions.
4. Keep separate [Dad](notebooks/dad/README.md) and [Son](notebooks/son/README.md) notebooks.
5. Compare predictions, record disagreements, and let evidence guide revisions.

Keep actual measurements separate from examples and simulated results. Record sources, units, uncertainty, and software versions. Preserve original observations even when they disagree with a prediction.
