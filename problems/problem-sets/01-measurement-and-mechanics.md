# Problem Set 1
Work independently. Show equations, units, and assumptions. All numbers are illustrative; none are measured flight data.

1. Convert 85 g to kg, 32 mm to m, and 250 cm to m.
2. A 0.120 kg rocket is airborne with instantaneous upward thrust 3.00 N. Ignore drag. Calculate weight, net force, acceleration, and T/W. What changes if upward-flight drag is 0.250 N?
3. With thrust equal to twice weight and zero drag, what is a/g? Explain.
4. Five mass readings are 81, 82, 81, 83, and 82 g. Find mean and range. Can you infer accuracy from these alone? Explain why range/2 is not automatically a 95% confidence interval.
5. Diameter is 25.0 ± 0.5 mm, a plausible bound. Calculate frontal area in m² and its approximate fractional bound. Calculate endpoint areas to check the approximation.
6. A 0.100 kg object receives constant net upward force 1.00 N for 0.20 s, starting at rest. Find acceleration and final velocity. Why can you not extend this constant-force model across an entire real rocket flight?
7. Someone says, “Thrust and exhaust reaction are equal, so the rocket has no net force.” Identify the incorrect system boundary.
8. Your simulation predicts 200 m, but an optical estimate is 180 ± 30 m. Is there strong evidence of a model error? What additional uncertainties or tests matter?

Before reviewing solutions, record one unresolved question and each learner’s confidence.
